# Module 1: Introduction to Cloud Computing

*Cloud computing is the on demand delivery of IT resources like servers, storage, and databases over the internet. 
Instead of buying and managing your own physical data centers, you can access these services from a provider like AWS on a pay as you-go basis.*

---

## 1. What is Cloud Computing?

*At its core, cloud computing is the on demand delivery of IT resources over the internet with pay as you go pricing. 
Think of it like electricity in your house. You don't own a power plant. You just plug into the wall (the internet) and pay a utility company (a cloud provider like AWS) only for the electricity you use.
Before the cloud, a company had to buy, build, and maintain its own "power plant",a physical data center with servers, storage, and networking hardware. This was expensive, slow, and inefficient.*

---

## 2. Key Benefits of the Cloud

* **Agility:** *Develop and deploy faster than any possible way.*
* **Cost Savings:** *Tarde massic Capital Expenses (CapEX) for variable Operational Expenses (OpEx). No need to buy server upfront.*
* **Elasticity:** *Handle spikes in traffic automatically. like Black Friday sales on many stores, the number of customers and orders are way more than usual.*
* **Innovation:** *Offload the undifferentiated heavy lifting of managing infrastructure to the cloud provider, allowing your teams to focus on building things that matter.*
* **Global Reach:** *Deploy your application in multiple region around the world with a few clicks*

---

## 3. The "as a Service" Models

* **IaaS (Infrastructure as a Service):**
    * **What it is:** *The cloud provider gives you the basic building block of the computing. You manage the operationg system and everything on it.*
    * **Analogy:** *You are leasing a raw kitchen space. You get the electricity, gas, and water, but you have to bring in your own ovens, counters, and ingredients.*
    * **Example:** Amazon EC2, Google Compute Engine.

* **PaaS (Platform as a Service):**
    * **What it is:** *The cloud provider manages the infrastructure and gives you the platform on which you can work on your application.*
    * **Analogy:** *You are leasing a fully equipped kitchen. The ovens and counters are already there. You just bring your ingredients and recipes.*
    * **Example:** AWS Elastic Beanstalk, Heroku.

* **SaaS (Software as a Service):**
    * **What it is:** *Everything will be managed by the cloud provider all you do is to use your software typically through a web browser.*
    * **Analogy:** *You are ordering a pizza from a restaurant. You don't cook, you don't manage the kitchen, you just eat the final product.*
    * **Example:** Gmail, Dropbox.

---

## 4. Cloud Deployment Models

* **Public Cloud:** *Services are offered over the public internet and available to anyone who wants to purchase them (e.g., AWS, Microsoft Azure, Google Cloud Platform). This is the most common model.*
* **Private Cloud:** *Computing resources are used exclusively by one business or organization. It can be located at the company’s on site data center or hosted by a third party service provider. This is for organizations that need high security or have strict regulatory requirements.*
* **Hybrid Cloud:** *A combination of public and private clouds, bound together by technology that allows data and applications to be shared between them. This gives businesses greater flexibility and more deployment options. You can take the example of a Bank that might run its core transaction system on a private cloud for security but use a public cloud for its public facing website and mobile app to handle variable traffic.*
